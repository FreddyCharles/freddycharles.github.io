How It Works

Lorentz Force & Magnetic Fields

Inside the tracker, a uniform axial magnetic field curves the trajectory of charged particles according to the Lorentz force equation: F = q(v × B). By measuring the radius of curvature, we can determine the particle's momentum. Neutral particles (Photons, Neutrinos) do not possess an electric charge and therefore travel in perfectly straight lines. Try adjusting the Magnetic Field slider to see how curvature reacts.

Calorimetry & Stopping Power

Detectors use dense materials to force particles to deposit their energy and stop. Electrons and Photons shower in the inner Electromagnetic Calorimeter (ECAL). Hadrons (protons, pions) are heavier and penetrate further, stopping in the Hadron Calorimeter (HCAL). Muons are unique—they are "minimum-ionizing" and pass through almost all matter, reaching the outermost Muon Spectrometer.

Quantum Decay Channels

When you fire the beam, the simulation probabilistically resolves different Standard Model events. Most often, quarks and gluons scatter into chaotic "jets" of hadrons. Occasionally, you will simulate a rare resonance decay, such as a Higgs Boson decaying into two high-energy photons (H → γγ), or a Z Boson decaying into two muons (Z → μ+ μ-).
